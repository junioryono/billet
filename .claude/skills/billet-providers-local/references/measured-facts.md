# Measured facts

Part of the `billet-providers-local` skill: what was measured, dated where it was recorded.

- Virtualization.framework refuses a third macOS guest and one under 4GiB; `config.DefaultMacOSVMLimit = 2` is pinned to that refusal.
- `tart get`: exit 2 `does not exist`; exit 1 `missing files for a supported layout`.
- `SendCtrlAltDel`: ignored by a real guest for twenty seconds.
- SIGTERM to a jailed VMM (a pid-namespace init with no handler): ignored for over two minutes; SIGKILL: immediate (2026-09-30).
- Delivery mechanisms in a real `ghcr.io/cirruslabs/ubuntu` guest: `nohup`, double fork, `systemd-run --user` all dead in three seconds; `setsid` survives.
- A node's serial queue: 300ms deadline, 60-second wait without `WaitDelay`.
- Docker `--filter name=` is a substring match.
- A container's own network counters are readable from the host without entering its namespace: `/proc/<container init pid>/net/dev` is the table of the container's namespace, readable as root and as uid 1000 from the host's pid namespace, and its `eth0` line counts from inside (rx is what the container received: 2098 bytes received against 651 sent after a download in it). A second network appears as `eth1`; the kernel's tunnel devices (`tunl0`, `gretap0`, ...) are listed in every namespace, and a name of seven or more characters has no space before its colon (Docker 29.4.3, kernel 6.12.76-linuxkit, 2026-10-08).

- **A tart VM's traffic is on its own `vmenet` interface, on both networks** (macos-01, macOS 27.0.1, tart 2.37.0, softnet 0.23.0, 2026-10-24, two `ubuntu-runner-arm64` VMs at once with the node stopped). Each VM adds one `vmenetN`, a member of `bridge100` (192.168.64.1): the NAT VM's and the softnet VM's alike. A 20,000,000-byte download inside the NAT guest moved 20,948,336 to 20,983,130 bytes out of its `vmenet` towards the guest (about 4.8% TCP/IP headers) and 156,583 in (the guest's ACKs); the host's out is the guest's received. `bridge100` counted every byte twice, so it is no per-VM count. The bridge's address table (`ifconfig bridge100 addr`) names each guest's MAC, the one in its tart `config.json`, against exactly one member, and `lsof` shows the VM's Virtualization.framework process (the NAT VM) or its softnet process (the softnet VM) holding a `com.apple.userspace_ethernet` control socket whose unit is the interface's number plus one; the guest chooses its own MAC, so the socket is the stronger tie. `tart ip` answered a stale lease (192.168.2.3) for a MAC whose current lease was 192.168.64.2: `/var/db/dhcpd_leases` held both. softnet must be on the PATH of whatever runs `tart run`, or the VM does not start ("softnet not found in PATH").
- **A Mac's CPU counters are readable by an ordinary user, without cgo** (macos-01, macOS 27.0.1, 18 cpus, 2026-10-24): `host_processor_info(PROCESSOR_CPU_LOAD_INFO)`, called through libSystem trampolines as `golang.org/x/sys/unix` calls libc, advanced 100 ticks per cpu-second (kern.clockrate's `stathz`, 100) and took 14 µs a read. The same call through the purego library also worked, at 0.3 MB more binary and a substitute cgo runtime for the whole process, so billet uses the trampolines (`internal/usage/hostcpu_darwin.go`).
- **The Mac's wall meter is read for its power, not its energy counter** (a Shelly Plug US Gen4 at 192.168.1.246, 2026-10-24): `Switch.GetStatus` answered `apower` in 0.1 W steps every second, 1.7 to 1.8 W with the Mac idle, while `aenergy.total` moved about once a minute in steps of about 0.12 Wh (430 J).
- **A running billet VM's link is named three ways that agree** (macos-01, macOS 27.0.1, tart 2.37.0, softnet 0.23.0, 2026-10-24, a softnet runner VM mid-job). `lsof -nP +w -a -p <softnet pid> -F n` printed `n[ctl com.apple.userspace_ethernet id 14 unit 1]`, the VM's own process held no such socket, the softnet's parent was `tart run <vm> --no-graphics --net-softnet --net-softnet-block=@host` and its `--vm-mac-address` the VM's `config.json` address, and `ifconfig bridge100 addr` had learned that address on `vmenet0`: unit 1 is vmenet0, as unit 2 was vmenet1 under the probe. The kernel's `NET_RT_IFLIST2` counters for `vmenet0` equalled `netstat -ibn`'s (35,551,570 bytes in, the guest's sent; 3,830,254,524 out, the guest's received). The interface was `Link#25`, the index a probe VM's `vmenet0` had held earlier that day, so a name and its index are reused from one VM to the next.
