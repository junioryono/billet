# Measured facts

Part of the `billet-providers-local` skill: what was measured, dated where it was recorded.

- Virtualization.framework refuses a third macOS guest and one under 4GiB; `config.DefaultMacOSVMLimit = 2` is pinned to that refusal.
- `tart get`: exit 2 `does not exist`; exit 1 `missing files for a supported layout`.
- `SendCtrlAltDel`: ignored by a real guest for twenty seconds.
- SIGTERM to a jailed VMM (a pid-namespace init with no handler): ignored for over two minutes; SIGKILL: immediate (2026-09-30).
- Delivery mechanisms in a real `ghcr.io/cirruslabs/ubuntu` guest: `nohup`, double fork, `systemd-run --user` all dead in three seconds; `setsid` survives.
- A node's serial queue: 300ms deadline, 60-second wait without `WaitDelay`.
- Docker `--filter name=` is a substring match.
- A container's own network counters are readable from the host without entering its namespace: `/proc/<container init pid>/net/dev` is the table of the container's namespace, readable as root and as uid 1000 from the host's pid namespace, and its `eth0` line counts from inside (rx is what the container received: 2098 bytes received against 651 sent after a download in it). A second network appears as `eth1`; the kernel's tunnel devices (`tunl0`, `gretap0`, ...) are listed in every namespace, and a name of seven or more characters has no space before its colon (Docker 29.4.3, kernel 6.12.76-linuxkit, 2026-10-08).

- **A tart VM's traffic is on its own `vmenet` interface, on both networks** (macos-01, macOS 27.0.1, tart 2.37.0, softnet 0.23.0, 2026-10-24, two `ubuntu-runner-arm64` VMs at once with the node stopped). Each VM adds one `vmenetN`, a member of `bridge100` (192.168.64.1): the NAT VM's and the softnet VM's alike. A 20,000,000-byte download inside the NAT guest moved 20,948,336 to 20,983,130 bytes out of its `vmenet` towards the guest (about 4.8% TCP/IP headers) and 156,583 in (the guest's ACKs); the host's out is the guest's received. `bridge100` counted every byte twice, so it is no per-VM count. The bridge's address table (`ifconfig bridge100 addr`) names each guest's MAC, the one in its tart `config.json`, against exactly one member, and `lsof` shows the VM's Virtualization.framework process (the NAT VM) or its softnet process (the softnet VM) holding a `com.apple.userspace_ethernet` control socket; here its unit was the interface's number plus one, which a later probe the same day showed is coincidence (below). `tart ip` answered a stale lease (192.168.2.3) for a MAC whose current lease was 192.168.64.2: `/var/db/dhcpd_leases` held both. softnet must be on the PATH of whatever runs `tart run`, or the VM does not start ("softnet not found in PATH").
- **A Mac's CPU counters are readable by an ordinary user, without cgo** (macos-01, macOS 27.0.1, 18 cpus, 2026-10-24): `host_processor_info(PROCESSOR_CPU_LOAD_INFO)`, called through libSystem trampolines as `golang.org/x/sys/unix` calls libc, advanced 100 ticks per cpu-second (kern.clockrate's `stathz`, 100) and took 14 µs a read. The same call through the purego library also worked, at 0.3 MB more binary and a substitute cgo runtime for the whole process, so billet uses the trampolines (`internal/usage/hostcpu_darwin.go`).
- **The Mac's wall meter is read for its power, not its energy counter** (a Shelly Plug US Gen4 at 192.168.1.246, 2026-10-24): `Switch.GetStatus` answered `apower` in 0.1 W steps every second, 1.7 to 1.8 W with the Mac idle, while `aenergy.total` moved about once a minute in steps of about 0.12 Wh (430 J).
- **A running billet VM's link: its softnet, that softnet's socket and the bridge's entry** (macos-01, macOS 27.0.1, tart 2.37.0, softnet 0.23.0, 2026-10-24, a softnet runner VM mid-job). `lsof -nP +w -a -p <softnet pid> -F n` printed `n[ctl com.apple.userspace_ethernet id 14 unit 1]`, the VM's own process held no such socket, the softnet's parent was `tart run <vm> --no-graphics --net-softnet --net-softnet-block=@host` and its `--vm-mac-address` the VM's `config.json` address, and `ifconfig bridge100 addr` had learned that address on `vmenet0`. The kernel's `NET_RT_IFLIST2` counters for `vmenet0` equalled `netstat -ibn`'s (35,551,570 bytes in, the guest's sent; 3,830,254,524 out, the guest's received). The interface was `Link#25`, the index a probe VM's `vmenet0` had held earlier that day, so a name and its index are reused from one VM to the next.
- **A socket's unit does not name its interface, and softnet drops a spoofed frame** (macos-01, macOS 27.0.1, tart 2.37.0, softnet 0.23.0, 2026-10-24, the node down, four and then one probe VM). With v0.26.0 deployed, eight real VMs in a row were refused because a socket's unit disagreed with the bridge: "link is vmenet4, but the bridges learned its address on [vmenet2]". Four probe VMs started and stopped across both networks were given control units 1, 2, 3, 4 and then 2 again, while their interfaces were vmenet0, vmenet1, then vmenet0, vmenet2, vmenet1 (lowest free); the bridge's table named the right member every time, a stopped VM's address left the table with its member, and `ifconfig -v` showed nothing that ties an interface to a socket. Each name kept its index and host-side address across VMs (`vmenet0` index 25, `be:de:de:94:0d:b3`, all day). A clone can keep its image's address (`86:46:87:ef:d0:52` on several VMs cloned from `ubuntu-runner-arm64`): of four cloned one after another, the first kept it and the other three were given new ones. A softnet guest that set its interface to `02:11:22:33:44:55` and sent was never learned under it, the bridge kept only its configured address, and the guest lost the internet (`curl` answered 000): softnet sends only the address it was told.
- **The node, as a launch agent, is held to macOS's Local Network privacy** (macos-01, macOS 27.0.1, 2026-10-24, v0.26.0). Started by `billet local up`, it read the Shelly plug at 192.168.1.246 as `connect: no route to host` on every tick, while `curl` from an SSH session reached the plug and the node reached the control plane on another subnet. The unified log showed `com.apple.networkextension` evaluating it each second as `LocalNetwork: found bundle id a.out by PID`: the release binary is ad-hoc signed with identifier `a.out` (`codesign -dv`), so macOS identified the node as `a.out` in its Local Network evaluation. The reads came back with no restart and no setting changed: a Screen Sharing connection (the Mac has no display) brought up the desktop of the account logged in at the console since 2026-10-17, `loginwindow` logged activity at 14:22:32 to 14:22:35 PDT, `nehelper` cached a UUID for `a.out` at 14:22:36.008, and the node logged `reading the power meter again` at 14:22:36, after failures from 13:17; afterwards System Settings, Privacy & Security, Local Network listed it, turned on (its state before is not known). With the screen locked again after Screen Sharing disconnected (`IOConsoleLocked` true at 14:27), the node kept reading, logging no failure. After an update it came back on its own: in the v0.26.0 to v0.27.0 rollout (completed 04:52:45 UTC 2026-10-25), the new node's startup line at 21:51:48 PDT (it names each VM's `vmenet`, as only v0.27.0's does) was followed in the same second by its first plug read refused with `connect: no route to host`, and at 21:51:49 by `reading the power meter again`, with nothing done at the Mac; whether the permission persisted or was granted again, the log does not say. What admitted it, and whether a logout blocks it, is not measured.
- **The Shelly plug answers in tens of milliseconds, and rarely in more than half a second** (macos-01 to a Shelly Plug US Gen4 on the same LAN, 2026-10-24, 1,033 reads twice a second over ten minutes with `curl`): median 41 ms, p90 49 ms, p99 70 ms, p99.9 106 ms, the slowest 338 ms, every answer a 200. The node's reads timed out at the 500 ms bound once in about an hour of one-a-second sampling (`context deadline exceeded` at 15:16:56 PDT, answered again at 15:16:57), which is why a failed reading is folded into the next rather than costing the jobs then running their energy.
- APFS `clonefile` (`cp -c`) leaves its source's device, inode, size and change time as they were (macOS 27.2, 2026-11-04), which a base's identity check rests on. GHCR answers an anonymous `GET /v2/<repo>/manifests/<tag>` with 401 and `Bearer realm="https://ghcr.io/token",service="ghcr.io",scope="repository:<repo>:pull"`, gives an anonymous token for it, and serves `application/vnd.oci.image.manifest.v1+json` whose sha256 is its `Docker-Content-Digest` (2026-11-04).
- A verification's path against the real tart 2.36.0 on macOS 27.2, in a private `TART_HOME`, with `ghcr.io/cirruslabs/debian` (635 MB compressed, 20 GB disk, 2026-11-04). `tart pull <repo>@<digest>` caches `cache/OCIs/ghcr.io/cirruslabs/debian/<digest>/manifest.json`, whose sha256 is that digest, beside a `.explicitly-pulled` marker. Its clone is standalone: `config.json`, `disk.img`, `nvram.bin`, no overlay, and config's `os` is `linux`. A name ending `.staging` is one tart accepts, and renaming a VM's directory and asking `tart get` under the new name works. A local `tart clone` leaves the source's three files' device, inode, size and change time as they were and moves only the directory's change time, and the clone's config differs by `macAddress` alone. A descriptor holding a flock passed to `tart run` stays held with the VM running 25 s later and frees once the process has ended; `lsof -t +w --` names exactly that process with no warning, and `ps -axww -o pid=,command=` shows it as `/opt/homebrew/Cellar/tart/2.36.0/libexec/tart.app/Contents/MacOS/tart run <name> --no-graphics`.
- `proc_pidpath` on macOS 27.2 (2026-11-04) reads the executable of a process another account owns, root's included, and answers `ESRCH` for one that has gone and for a zombie, so the census skips those and refuses on anything else.
- **A guest agent answers 30 to 40 seconds after tart lists the VM, and a `tart exec` before that blocks 30 seconds and fails** (a development Mac, M2 Max, macOS 26.0, tart 2.36.0, ubuntu-runner-arm64, 2026-10-25, three boots): the first exec succeeded 30.7, 40.5 and 30.8 seconds after `tart list` reported the VM running, and each failed exec blocked 30.0 to 30.1 seconds before printing "Failed to connect to the VM using its control socket … is the Tart Guest Agent running?". A guest five seconds into its boot took 17.6 seconds to report itself stopped after `tart stop`.
- **A `tart delete` killed part-way leaves a directory no inventory can read** (the same Mac, 2026-10-25): the stray cleanup's thirty-second bound killed `tart delete` of a VM stopped moments before, and left `config.json`, `nvram.bin`, `control.sock` and `billet-owner` without `disk.img`; `tart get` answered "VM is missing files for a supported layout" and `tart list` omitted it. A delete killed within 0.2 seconds of starting, of a VM that had never run, left nothing, so the window is the deletion of a disk the guest wrote to.
- **softnet with `@host` keeps a guest from the Mac's own services** (the same Mac, softnet 0.23.0, 2026-10-25): a listener on `0.0.0.0:18722` answered a guest on tart's default NAT at its gateway `192.168.64.1` and at the Mac's LAN address, and answered neither from a guest booted with `--net-softnet --net-softnet-block=@host` (gateway `192.168.2.1`), which reached the internet.
- **A misreading, kept as the record of it: `tart run` writing into its own VM bundle looked like what made an ASIF macOS guest fail to start** (a fleet Mac, macOS 27.2 26B123, tart 2.41.1, softnet 0.25.0, `ghcr.io/cirruslabs/macos-golden-gate-xcode:27.2` on an ASIF disk, 2026-11-07): every billet launch failed with `VZErrorDomain Code=1 "The virtual machine failed to start." … Internal Virtualization error.` at 6 vCPU/24 GiB and at 2/4, before and after v0.31.0, while identical commands by hand booted it every time. A probe reproducing billet's start with each difference switchable, one fresh VM a case, failed 3 of 4 with all of billet's differences (stdout and stderr appended to `billet-run.log` inside the bundle, the parent waiting on `tart run`, `tart list` polled from the start, an flocked fd 3) and booted 3 of 3 with only the log moved outside the bundle; dropping only the wait or only the polling also booted (1 of 1 each), and the fd made no difference. The failures went on with the log moved (v0.32.0); the probe's small samples had caught a race whose real trigger is the next entry. macOS 26.5 and the arm64 Linux image on raw disks were never affected.
- **`tart list` during an ASIF disk's attach fails the start** (the same Mac, golden-gate 27.2 at 6 vCPU and 24 GiB, 2026-11-07). tart 2.41.1 sizes every VM in `tart list` and `tart get`, and an ASIF disk's size comes from `diskutil image info --plist` on it (List.swift, VMDirectory.diskSizeBytes, Diskutil.imageInfo); `tart run` takes the pid lock that makes `tart list` say running before it calls the framework's start (Run.swift). With `diskutil image info` looped every 0.3 s for 60 s beside a fresh `tart run`, judged by `tart run` still up and `tart exec true` answering: from the start, 2 of 2 failed with `Internal Virtualization error.`; from the framework's process first holding the disk (0.2 to 0.4 s, the only holder, for ASIF and raw alike), 1 of 3; and, with billet's node stopped so that nothing else listed, from 5, 15 or 30 s after the holder, from `tart ip --wait` answering (5.6 to 6.5 s) and from the guest's first `tart exec` answer (13.3 to 15.5 s), 0 of 3 each, as with no polling at all (0 of 3).
- **A Tart `@verified` verification of the documented macOS image passes on the reference Mac, from an SSH shell** (a fleet Mac, billet v0.34.0, tart 2.41.1, macOS 27.2, 2026-11-08): `billet images verify ghcr.io/cirruslabs/macos-golden-gate-xcode:27.2`, run as the node's user over SSH, pulled `@sha256:d7641b1d…` (77.0 GB compressed) into its private store, recorded base `billetbase-<deployment>-fb60e5e49630-1` (runner 2.338.2, darwin guest) and exited 0. So that image is not stacked, and a verification needs no GUI session.
- **A guest's slot or token is held by its `tart run` alone, never by softnet** (the same Mac, the same day, an untrusted Linux guest under `--net-softnet --net-softnet-block=@host`): `lsof` showed `~/.tart/.billet/guest-token-<vm name>` open as fd 3 in the `tart run` process the node started, and its softnet child (`--vm-fd 0`) held neither a `guest-token-` nor a `macos-slot-` descriptor. So a slot or token lives exactly as long as the VM's own process. On a macOS guest the same holds for its slot (the same Mac, 13:49 UTC, an untrusted macOS 27 job under softnet beside a Linux guest): its `tart run` held `~/.tart/.billet/macos-slot-1` as fd 3, its softnet held neither a slot nor a token, the Linux guest's `tart run` held its `guest-token-` and no slot, and `billet check` reported one of two macOS slots held and no uncoordinated guest.
