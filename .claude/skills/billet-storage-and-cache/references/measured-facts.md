# Measured facts

Part of the `billet-storage-and-cache` skill: what was measured, dated where it was recorded.

- ZFS clone reachable from one machine only; RBD clone mapped from a second kernel client.
- Clone + map + mount: ~350ms for 10GiB; sequential reads 4.5GB/s at depth; 4k sync writes 14x slower than local on fio; real builds 1.02x, 2.36x, 4.07x.
- `rbd info -weirdpool/x`: `unrecognised option`; `client.billet` with a `client.` prefix authenticates as `client.client.billet`.
- The destination pool's `rbd_default_clone_format` governs a clone.
- `CreateSnapshot` + `ClientToken`: `UnknownParameter` (HTTP 400), every settlement failed, every job ran cold (2026-09-03).
- A failed writer lease held its key fifteen minutes; the waiting writer polled about twenty times a second for six minutes.
- Site acceptance (2026-09-03): two nodes at one site reuse one generation, a second site starts cold and cannot reach the first's store, and the same label falls back across sites.
- An interrupted unmap blocklists the whole host (2026-10-03, Ceph 20.2.0). Root disks are clones of a guest image with `exclusive-lock`, which the kernel holds while one is mapped, and every device on a host shares one kernel client. A node told to drain cancelled its context with `rbd device unmap` in flight; the kernel logged `failed to unlock header: -512` and kept the lock and the watch. Twenty-eight seconds later the root disk's `trash mv` broke the orphaned lock, and under `rbd_blocklist_on_break_lock=true`, Ceph's default, that blocklisted the kernel client: every mapped device returned -108 and every map refused with `(108) Cannot send after transport endpoint shutdown` until the entry was removed by hand. So `unmapDevice` runs each unmap on `context.WithoutCancel` under `MapTimeout`, and the root disk's `trash mv` and the purge's `trash rm` pass `--rbd_blocklist_on_break_lock false` (`noBlocklist`). The rbd CLI accepts the option in both the `=` and the two-token form and refuses an unknown one.
- An advisory lock's `lock rm` blocklisted a client that no longer existed, at every release (2026-11-10, Ceph 20.2.0, #845). The locker an advisory lock records is the client of the `rbd lock add` that took it, and that CLI exits at once, so the entry fenced nothing: the holder's later commands are new clients. Traced on ubuntu-01 with bpftrace over 150 s, `rbd lock rm` on `billet-cache/.cache-index` ran 54 times beside 56 `osd blocklist add` entries from short-lived `client.billet` sessions, about 35 OSD map epochs a minute with about 1,060 entries listed; on a throwaway image the same day, a default `lock rm` added one entry naming the locker's address and `--rbd_blocklist_on_break_lock false lock rm` removed the lock and added none. Every `lock rm`, billet's and the guest-image build script's, now passes the option.
- A lost `lock add` answer can still take the lock (2026-10-03, Ceph 20.2.0). On a loaded node, a cache-index `lock add` killed at the 15-second rbd bound was committed by the cluster after the attempt had listed the lockers, found none of its own, and failed. The lock then had an owner nobody claimed and no heartbeat, so every cache operation on the site (Docker image stores, CAS attaches, renewals) waited from 22:45 until the stale bound broke it near 22:57. Guests reached GitHub minutes late and started cold. `takeLock` now records every cookie it gives up with its fate unknown (an add answering anything but success, 16 or 2, and a `Release` whose listing or removal failed after the heartbeat stopped) in `Client.abandoned`. A later attempt that finds the lock held under such a cookie removes it at once, whatever its age, because the nonce makes it nobody else's. A process restart forgets the record, and the stale bound remains the fallback.
- Deleting an RBD image without object-map removes every object its size could hold (2026-10-05, Ceph 20.2.0, the reference deployment's Linux node under load). A 100 GiB volume with 100 MiB written took 237 s to delete with `layering` alone and 3.5 s with `layering,exclusive-lock,object-map,fast-diff,deep-flatten`; a clone given those features of a `layering`-only parent took 11 s. Cache volumes were created with `layering` alone and clones inherit their parent's features, so the node's trash purge, four `trash rm` at once, kept both OSDs near 99% busy (about 1,900 writes/s and 225 MB/s each while guests wrote 16 MiB/s) and every job's IO waited behind it for hours.
- A purge that re-asked every held trash entry and every live generation each pass cost about 18 W of package power at idle (2026-10-12, Ceph 20.2.0, the reference deployment's Linux node, no microVM running). The cache pool's trash held 1,364 retired writers whose generations were live and the pool listed 1,395 generations; each pass, one about every ninety seconds, ran `rbd trash rm` on every held entry (each refused ENOTEMPTY, deleting two or three a pass) and `rbd info` on every generation older than an hour. The host forked about 1,400 processes a second (`processes` in `/proc/stat`, threads included). Over 240 one-second samples, the 226 seconds with more than 300 forks averaged 94.8 W of RAPL package power and the 14 seconds between passes 76.9 W, against the 73.5 W idle measured on 2026-09-25; the 60 s idle read that day was 87.6 W at 1.5% busy. It also failed the known-answer energy check's idle baseline (86.0 and 85.7 W at the quiet ends against 73.5 W ± 5%).
- A compaction published a damaged cache generation (2026-10-05, found 2026-10-13, Ceph 20.2.3, e2fsprogs 1.47.2, the reference deployment's Linux node). The Go key's generation `1791233900-7f26324f3e5fd2d379cf1af8` was an `rbd cp` (lineage 0, no parent) made three minutes after another compaction timed out and across a node restart; `rbd du` showed 4 MiB of data, `e2fsck -f -n` answered 12 ("The journal superblock is corrupt") and a read-only mount failed with EUCLEAN. 298 Go builds a day ran uncached. The same `rbd cp` of a healthy cloned generation on a quiet cluster copied all 552.8 MiB and passed e2fsck in 3 s, so the copy is not wrong by construction; what it produced was never checked. THAT WAS THE WRONG CONCLUSION, by a measurement of a chain that did not show it: see the next line.
- `rbd cp` of a snapshot of a clone with object-map silently loses what the clone's parents hold (2026-11-04, Ceph 20.2.0-0ubuntu2.1 client and v20.2.0 cluster, kernel 7.0.0-1016-aws, e2fsprogs 1.47.2, one host; the generic 7.0.0-38 kernel the reference node runs would not boot on that instance). A parent holding 244 MiB and a clone writing 8 MiB: the clone's snapshot used 24 MiB, the copy used 24 MiB and differed from it, e2fsck answered 4. Bytes read through librbd and the kernel agreed, so the data is absent rather than misread: in a replay of the store's own commands, 204 objects of a 20 GiB image at depth 8 were zeros in the copy and data in its source, e2fsck 12 or 4, with the seven clones before it clean. Whole: the same chain with `layering` or `layering,exclusive-lock` on the clone, whatever its parent carries, and the head of the same clone. Replacements from that stage: `rbd clone` and `rbd flatten` 3.0 s and 980 MiB; `rbd deep cp --flatten` 42.7 s and 40 GiB; `rbd deep cp` alone keeps the parent, so compacts nothing. With the clone and the flatten, the same replay ran 20 rounds through two compactions, formatted the node's way and the guest's, every candidate clean; rbd cloned and flattened chains of 8 to 24 levels, whole, and the kernel mapped them read-only.
- Holders and mounts say nothing about a live RBD mapping on a node (2026-10-16, Ceph 20.2.3, the reference deployment's Linux node, read-only). `rbd device list` held four `billet-<lease>` root disks and four `cache-v-*` volumes, each volume named by exactly one cache custody record; for every one `/sys/block/rbdN/holders` was empty and the host's `/proc/mounts` listed nothing, because the node's unit mounts in a mount namespace of its own and each VMM opens its device inside its jail. Only the kernel's refusal of a non-forced unmap tells a held mapping from a leaked one.
- A node that closed an npm answer's connection on its last byte crashed `pnpm/setup` v3.1.2 on a warm answer (2026-10-26, Node 24.19 in the reference deployment's guests; `node:24-bookworm` containers through the real node and guest relay on the shared test host). On the reference deployment, ten of the eleven Platform jobs that set up pnpm through a warm cache failed with `SyntaxError: Expecting Unicode escape sequence \uXXXX (1:15130)`, from 09:59 to 10:33 UTC. With `--stack-trace-limit=200` the frames under it were Node's `assert.ok` called by the bundled undici's `finish()` (`J(!this.paused)`) from the TLS socket's `end`. On the test host, ten warm runs of the action at a time, four rounds each way: 22 of 40 failed with that error when the node closed first, and none of 40 when it waited for the client's close. With the job's container held open five seconds past its client, every answered connection was closed by its client within 50 ms (26 to 46 ms, the slowest of each job's five).
- EC2 accepts `DeleteVolume` on a volume still `creating`, and its tags are already there (2026-11-04, us-east-1 and us-west-2: three empty 1 GiB gp3 volumes, and four from an encrypted 8 GiB snapshot, each gone within 6 s). So the ebs-s3 store removes a volume whose wait its caller gave up on without waiting for it (`discardUnansweredVolume`).
- A store volume per Actions archive on EBS, 2026-11-04, `c7i.2xlarge` in `us-west-2a`, gp3 at the store's settings under a KMS key: 20.3 to 20.5 s from `CreateVolume` to mounted, a lazy read of 1 GiB in 27.6 to 57.4 s and of 4 GiB in 87.3 to 209.3 s, 332.5 s to snapshot a volume with 5 GiB used, 16.8 s to detach; `MaximumEbsAttachments` 32 on `c7i.large`, `c7i.2xlarge`, `c7i.4xlarge` and `m7i.xlarge`. S3 from the same host through a gateway endpoint: 1 GiB in 11.3 to 20.8 s on one stream, 1.4 s on 8 and 0.9 s on 16; 4 GiB in 62.7 to 76.6 s, 9.4 to 11.3 s and 4.5 to 5.1 s. GitHub's blob store from the same host, read through a signed artifact URL: 1 GiB in 107 to 119 s, 18.8 to 19.1 s and 12.6 to 20.9 s; 4 GiB in 419 s, 64.3 to 72.7 s and 41.4 to 47.5 s ([EC2 cache probes](../../../../docs/reference/records/ec2-cache-probes.md)).
- A self-attached cache volume on AWS metal, 2026-11-10, `z1d.metal` in `us-west-2a` (the bare-metal hosts record): create and attach of an empty 1 GiB volume 3.9 s; verify, detach and snapshot 59.2 s; clone and attach 3.7 s; mount and first read 19 ms; detach and delete 11.6 s. On the same host an RBD clone of 1 GiB took 0.04 to 0.06 s and its kernel map 0.06 to 0.07 s. The acceptance matrix's seven caches ran cold, then warm, on the pool host; the Docker image store attached in 3.8 to 11.3 s cold and 3.8 to 6.4 s warm for seven launches at once, and a sticky disk's or builder's publication took 55 to 79 s of the job's post step.
