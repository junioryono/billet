# A store of whole objects, on EC2

Part of the `billet-storage-and-cache` skill. Each rule is stated in full, with the measurement or incident behind it. [ADR-023](../../../../docs/reference/decisions/adr-023-caches-off-firecracker.md) holds the decision and [EC2 cache probes](../../../../docs/reference/records/ec2-cache-probes.md) the numbers.

**On `ebs-s3`, a cache whose unit is a file is objects, not volumes, and that was measured.** `store.ArchiveStore` and `store.BlobStore` are optional capabilities of the store contract, and the node asks for them (`CacheService.archiveStore`, `blobStore`) before it reaches for a volume, so Ceph and Firecracker take the path they always took. On 2026-11-04, from a `c7i.2xlarge` through a gateway endpoint, a volume per archive cost 20 s before its first byte, 18 to 47 MiB/s lazily reading from its snapshot, and 332 s of snapshot per save, against an instance's 32 attachments; S3 read 360 to 730 MiB/s on 8 streams with no wait, and GitHub's own store gave the same host 54 to 64 MiB/s on 8. Sticky disks and the Docker image store stay EBS: they are filesystems a job mounts. `max_size` bounds what one job adds here, because there is no volume to fill, and retention bounds what a key holds.

**The guest half is the firecracker agent's own text, cut out at build time.** `billet ami build` runs `scripts.GuestImageBlock` over `scripts/build-guest-image.sh` and installs the regions as functions in `/usr/local/lib/billet/guest-caches.sh`, with `fetch` answered from what the boot script exported, so a guest has one mechanism however it boots. A change to a marked region changes both images. The region's text may reach for MMDS only through `fetch`, and the two tests in `scripts/guestimageblock_test.go` hold that. On this image the passthroughs tunnel through `node-relay`, which does the TLS: nesting a client's TLS inside a second session is what Python's sockets cannot do, and the node sees the same CONNECT it sees from any guest. A relay that did not start leaves interception off rather than half on, and the node's `opaqueTLSListener` hides the listener's TLS from `http.Server` so the HTTP/2 with prior knowledge that arrives through the relay is served at all.

**A commit's condition decides the race, and asks the object when its answer is lost.** `CompleteMultipartUpload` carries `If-None-Match: *`, so of two uploads of one key the first wins and the second is told `published: false`. An archive never changes, so there is no pointer, no writer lease and no fence. The upload's name is on the object as metadata, because a completion that lost its answer may have landed, and the only way to tell your own from another writer's is to ask: `CommitArchive` asks first on every call, since a retry after a restart finds its blocks gone. S3 answers a part copy and a completion `200` and can have failed, in the body, so both read it (`TestAPartCopyThatAnswers200AndFailedIsAFailure`, `TestACompletionThatAnswers200AndFailedIsAmbiguousNotSuccess`). A block below S3's 5 MiB part minimum anywhere but last cannot be a part, so such an upload is read back and rejoined (`Store.rejoin`), and the toolkit's own 64 MiB blocks never need that (`saveCacheV2`, read 2026-11-04).

**A read leaves no trace on an object, so retention is written down: a marker, or a record.** An archive's lookup writes a small marker (`archive-used/`), and eviction takes one neither written nor looked up within the retention. A download cannot lose its archive, because the retention is days and a job is hours, so no lease is needed to say so. A content-addressed entry is read by the thousand, so a job records what it used once, at the end (`RecordBlobUse`), and eviction is a mark and a sweep over those records; a record that cannot be read stops the sweep, because what it names cannot be told from what nobody uses. Staged blocks and unfinished multipart uploads go after 48 hours, longer than any job, and an unfinished upload appears in no object listing and bills all the same, so `Purge` asks for them by name. A download does not normally lose its archive, because the retention is days and a job is hours. One looked up in the moment an eviction pass takes it fails, which the client reports as a miss, and the read is held to the object it matched (`If-Match` on the listing's ETag, `TestAReadNeverMixesTwoArchivesOfOneKey`), so a key saved again meanwhile cannot hand it half of each. A use record is split at `blobUseEntries` and read to `blobUseLimit`, and a record past that limit stops the sweep rather than ending where the reader stopped, because what it did not reach would read as unused.

**A relay that started proves nothing about the node, so the entry point asks before anything names it.** The relay reports ready without dialling, and the next steps rewrite `https://github.com/` to it, which `git` does not fall back from. An image built without the listener's issuer, or a security group that drops the listener, would then fail every checkout, which is the one thing a cache may never do. So `billet_guest_caches_probe` runs `curl` against the endpoint with a bound and no credential, any HTTP answer proves it, and could-not-tell stops the relay and clears the endpoint, which turns off everything that names it (`TestANodeThatDoesNotAnswerTurnsTheCachesOff`). The order is the mechanism, and `TestTheEntryPointRunsTheGuestCachesInOrder` holds it.

**A TLS socket can be readable with nothing for the application, and the relay's handshake is part of its dial.** A Go listener with tickets on sends one in its first flight. OpenSSL does not read it during the handshake, so `select` calls the socket readable, and a blocking read consumes the ticket and waits on, leaving the client unserviced: a request past one read then stalls against a node waiting for the rest of it, until the 300-second idle bound (`TestALargeRequestIsNotHeldUpByARecordWithNoData`). So `read_ready` reads a TLS socket without blocking, and the listener also turns tickets off. A restarted node binds its port before it serves, so a dial succeeds at once and a ClientHello waits unanswered in the queue, where a plain client's request would simply wait: `dial_node` retries the handshake as it retries the dial, within the same 600 seconds, and stops at once on a certificate that fails verification, because that node is the node it will go on being.

**Docker on this image may have read its configuration before the boot changed it.** The AMI enables Docker at boot, unlike a Firecracker guest, and the prepare region writes `dns` into `daemon.json` for the container resolver. Only the path that mounts a store stops and starts the daemon, and `systemctl start` on a running service does nothing, so a cold path would leave npm interception on with a daemon that never read the resolver, and a container on a user-defined network would be answered that the registry is its own loopback, which is the failure the region's comment records as measured. The entry point tells the helper (`BILLET_DOCKER_CONFIG_CHANGED`) and a cold path restarts instead. A Firecracker guest is told nothing and starts Docker as it always has.

**What one job can make a node hold is bounded per node, not per archive.** `readAheadReaders` reads at once fetch ahead, each holding at most five 8 MiB chunks, and a read past that is one plain stream that holds nothing and is only slower. The bound is the node's 128 connections against a node that may have 4 GiB: a job can open a signed URL that many times and stop reading. A job's writes on its directory are bounded by `max_size` as a whole allowance: an object past what is left is refused whole, writes side by side settle up after each is stored, and recovery counts what is already in the directory (`writtenBytes`), so a restart does not refill it.

**An upload holds its session, so sessions upload side by side, and a commit does not hold its session at all.** A publication window counts from the job's completion, so `RetryClosed` finishes up to `closedSessionUploads` sessions at once on a store of objects, and one job's output does not spend the next job's window; on any other store it is one at a time, as it always was. The kill switch is asked again every `casPolicyAge` during an upload. `publishObjectReceipt` gives up `session.mu` around `CommitArchive`, because small blocks are read back through the node for minutes and BuildKit saves its layers side by side: two finalizations of one receipt are safe, because the store's condition lets one land and the other reads the object back as its own, and a session finished meanwhile has no record to raise again. Asking whether an entry is there costs no request (`reapi.Sizer`, from the listing), and a `FindMissingBlobs` of a build's thousands would otherwise download each one. The read cache is reaped every minute, because a build fills it in minutes and the fill exists to keep a job from meeting the line where writes refuse.

**A rejoin compares what it read with what it staged.** `io.ErrUnexpectedEOF` ends a block shorter than the buffer and a body cut short alike, so only the length tells them apart. A short archive published would stay: the first writer holds the key, every restore fails to extract, and each one refreshes the marker that keeps it from eviction (`TestARejoinThatReadsABlockShortPublishesNothing`).
