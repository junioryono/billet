# The upgrade root, its claim and the converge guard

Part of the `billet-releases-and-upgrades` skill. Each rule is stated in full, with the measurement or incident behind it.

**The claim is one name with three shapes, and a converge holds it as a directory.** `internal/ops/host/convergeguard.go`: `billet converge-guard hold|release|status|recover|holder`. `active` a symlink is a Go transaction, a regular file a pre-R role transaction, a DIRECTORY holding `guard.json` (holder, claimed_at, hostname, release_executable, release_executable_sha256) a converge guard, a directory without the record an `unpublished-guard`; `classifyClaim` reads it by `Lstat` with no lock and creates nothing, and a directory was chosen because every pre-R reader refuses one and none can release it. Publication under the transaction lock, in one order the fs hook (`guardHook`, operations identified by descriptor metadata) asserts: `Lstat(active)` absent, `mkdir`, create `guard.json.tmp` (`O_EXCL|O_NOFOLLOW|O_NONBLOCK`), write, fsync, rename, fsync `active`, fsync the root (the root's parent is flushed by EVERY acquisition of the lock, right after the root is found or made, because an acquisition that made it and died before its flush leaves a root the next one finds existing). Release: unlink the record, fsync `active`, `rmdir`, fsync the root; refused while `active/recovery` (the role's transaction pointer) exists, dangling included (`Lstat`, never `Stat`). A same-holder hold validates (mode, owner, no stale tmp, a `--candidate` equal to the recorded executable) and touches nothing, asserted by inode and mtime; another holder refuses by exact name with its age, and NOTHING EXPIRES A GUARD (`billet check` warns past `guardStaleAfter`, a day). `--candidate` is a file inside a `recovery-*` direct child of the root, opened relative to the validated root's descriptor (`openat`, `O_NOFOLLOW`), examined on its descriptor, hashed through it, and the name checked against the descriptor afterwards (`guardAfterHash` is the seam a test swaps files under); else the managed binary the same way. The recorded executable is verified by digest in `status --json` and NEVER RUN. A takeover (`hold --recover-from OLD --old-driver-stopped`) needs the guard to name OLD exactly, to carry a pointer whose journal reads, and a clean process scan; it rewrites the record through a fresh tmp created exclusively, an existing one examined through an identity descriptor (regular, owned, one link) and removed by name first, keeping the pointer and the executable. `recover --holder` removes a pointerless guard under the same assertion and scan; `recover --unpublished` removes a directory holding nothing but regular `guard.json.tmp` files, every entry examined before any is removed. The scan (`convergeguard_scan.go`, `guardProcesses` seam, `/proc` on Linux, unsupported on darwin) checks the scanner's own ancestry first with its own diagnostic, then every process for `AnsiballZ_` or `ansible-tmp-`, and an unreadable table or entry is could-not-tell. Every transaction entry classifies the claim IMMEDIATELY AFTER THE LOCK: `startHostUpgradeHolding` before darwin's `checkBinaryDir` seam and the `resolveRelease` seam, `resumeHostUpgrade` before any journal, and `hostUpgradeFromRollout` takes the lock FIRST and holds one lock from classification through `settleOnTarget` (the `timerBarrier` seam observes it held at the instruction read and at settlement), exiting 0 with "guarded by H since T; nothing to do" on a guarded host and "nothing to decide" under a held lock; a guarded host's refusal is `guarded by H since T (HOSTNAME)`, carried by the node's `AckRefused` as the retryable refusal the coordinator records. `publishClaim`, `releaseClaim` and `abandonClaim` never remove a claim that is not a Go transaction's symlink. `billet status` prints `guard` when one is held, `billet check` warns on a stale, unpublished or unreadable one, and `host-upgrade --status` prints `claim <shape>` for a non-symlink or dangling claim. THE PREPARATION IS ONE COMMAND UNDER ONE LOCK (`convergeguardprepare.go`, `billet converge-guard prepare --holder H --json` with `--validate`, `--candidate C [--allow-downgrade]`, `--no-change`, `--recovery`, `--dry-run`, `--expect-bootstrap`, `--expect-id`; the flag table refused before anything is opened): the record's eight members add `id` (public, 128 bits from `crypto/rand`, minted at publication, adopted onto a five-member record by the first `--validate`), `token` (the acquirer's, printed only in the `acquired` answer) and `preparing` (true from acquisition to `settle --token`); the pointer validated under the lock, the recorded executable verified by digest on every mutating branch, the owed flushes completed on readmission, a stray `guard.json.tmp` removed first, the judgement (capability through the candidate's `prepare --dry-run --json`, judged by `judgeDryRunAnswer` as the dry-run report naming the held guard's own id and never through `status`, which the guard's first commits answered without a `prepare` to follow, the downgrade through `version.Compare`) before any re-binding; `release --cleanup --token` only for a preparing guard with that token and no pointer (a settled one answers "the preparation window has closed"); refusals exit 2 with a reason code, could-not-tell exit 3; `--dry-run` takes no lock and reports every shape. The crash seam `convergeguardgatecrash.go` (`//go:build billetgatecrash`, `BILLET_GUARD_CRASH_AT="<kind> <path suffix>[:n]"`, exit 137 before the operation) is compiled into the gate's binaries and no shipped one, held to that by a structural test. `tests/fixtures/guard-prepare/*.json` and `guard-status/*.json` are written by the Go tests (`BILLET_UPDATE_FIXTURES=1`, deterministic ids through `deterministicGuardRandom`) and are what every fake answers from.

**The upgrade root's trust boundary is proved through descriptors before the lock, and again under it.** `takeTxLock`: the root's parent is reached by `openTrustedDir`, an ANCHORED WALK from `/` with each component opened `openat` relative to the descriptor of the one above it, `O_DIRECTORY|O_NOFOLLOW`, a link component opened as itself (`regularfile.OpenIdentityAt`), required to be a link OWNED BY ROOT OR BY THE EXPECTED ACCOUNT (a sticky directory keeps another account from renaming billet's entries and says nothing about a link that account made there, which it can repoint), then read with `readlinkat` and its target walked the same way with its components AS WRITTEN, a `..` opened where it stands after the links before it (macOS spells `/var` as a link; at most `maxTrustedLinks` hops), and every directory on the way judged by `requireTrustedAncestor` (owned by root or by `guardExpectedOwner`, and group/other-writable only under the sticky bit, because a writable ancestor lets another account rename the whole chain); the parent itself owned by `guardExpectedOwner`, the process's euid, and not group/other-writable; the root `Lstat`ed (created with `mkdirat` inside the parent when absent, refused as a symlink) and THE PARENT FLUSHED BY EVERY ACQUISITION right after, opened `openat` relative to the parent and judged on the descriptor (owned, not group/other-writable; the role establishes it 0700 and a test's `TempDir` is 0755, which is why the rule is "nobody else can write it" rather than one mode); the lock opened `openat` relative to the root with a create-or-open loop (`openLockFile`: a single `O_CREAT` open answered ENOENT to a racing loser on APFS, measured 2026-09-09), an EXISTING lock opened identity first (`regularfile.OpenAt`, `O_PATH` on Linux and `O_SYMLINK` elsewhere, so a link at the name is the link's own inode and refused as not regular on every platform) so a device at its name is refused without its driver, and required a regular file owned by the account; and once the flock is held, `requireRootInPlace` requires the root's NAME to still hold the directory the lock was taken inside (`Lstat` by name, `SameFile` with `txLock.dir`), and a resume or a takeover reads the journal THROUGH THE ROOT (`readJournalUnder`: the claim's target a direct child of the root, opened relative to `txLock.dir`, the journal read relative to that by `hostupgrade.ReadJournalAt`), with the claim's target required to be EXACTLY `<root>/<name>` by its text (`recoveryChild`: no `.`, `..` or separator, nothing cleaned first, because `<root>/hop/../x` is `<root>/x` to a lexical check and `/elsewhere/x` to the kernel), the recovery directory judged on its descriptor (owned, writable by nobody else) and the journal on its own (`ReadJournalAt`'s trust callback), because a journal reachable through a second name is a step and a generation another account chooses and a writable recovery directory lets a journal's removal read as a transaction that never began; as the Go transaction's own claim helpers (`publishClaim`, `releaseClaim`, `abandonClaim`) act relative to the root descriptor they are handed, so a root displaced at its name after the lock resolves nothing; what still goes by name is the transaction's own journal writing under the lock (`internal/hostupgrade`) and the abandoned tree's removal, both bounded to a direct child of the root. Everything a holder opens under the root opens relative to `txLock.dir`, and the guard directory a mutator acts on is opened, validated (0700, owned) and classified through one descriptor (`openGuardForMutation`) before its record is believed; the record and a candidate are opened identity first (`regularfile.OpenAt`) and THE RECORD IS JUDGED ON ITS OWN DESCRIPTOR (`requireTrustedFile`: regular, owned, writable by nobody else), because a record hard-linked from a file another account owns is one that account can rewrite whatever the directory's mode says; a stale `guard.json.tmp` is examined through an identity descriptor (regular, owned, one link) and then REMOVED by name relative to the directory and re-created exclusively, never opened for writing and never truncated; a symlink claim whose target cannot be examined for any reason but ENOENT is could-not-tell, never dangling; a record that cannot be read names no holder, so `holder`, `release`, `recover --holder` and a takeover refuse over it; and the timer's `--from-rollout` exits 0 only over a recognised guard (`errHostGuarded`), a claim that could not be classified being the failure it is. Measured 2026-09-09 on both platforms: `O_NOFOLLOW` on a symlink to a file or a directory is ELOOP, `O_NOFOLLOW|O_DIRECTORY` on a symlink to a directory is ENOTDIR, parent-component symlinks are followed (which is why every component is opened relative to a descriptor), a FIFO opened `O_WRONLY|O_NONBLOCK` with no reader is ENXIO, dup'd and inherited descriptors share a flock and `LOCK_UN` on any releases all.

**A version line names a build or the read is an error.** `executableVersion` (internal/ops/host/convergeguardprepare.go) reads the FIRST LINE alone of `<binary> version`, requires `billet <token> ...`, and takes the token as a release (`version.Canonical`) or as one of the COMPLETE development forms the version package prints (`(devel)`, `(unknown)`; a Go pseudo-version judged by Go's own parser, `golang.org/x/mod/module.IsPseudoVersion`, in exactly the three shapes `cmd/go` mints, with the twelve-hex revision required on top because x/mod admits any length, and `module.PseudoVersionTime` and `module.PseudoVersionBase` required to answer on the token without its build suffix, because the shape check admits a month of thirteen and a `vX.Y.0-0.` base with no predecessor (round 11); a dirty checkout at a release tag, `vX.Y.Z+dirty`, or any build suffix on a release base, which the version package passes through and Canonical refuses; a GoReleaser snapshot `X.Y.Z-SNAPSHOT-<hash>`; every one a valid semantic version); a hand-written regexp admitted `v0.10.1-<stamp>-<hash>` (the timestamp-only form needs a `vX.0.0` base) and `v0.10.1-rc10.<stamp>-<hash>` (a prerelease base needs its own `.0.`), which both read as no release to compare, and refused the dirty tag, which blocked every candidate (round 10, 2026-09-10); a prefix alone or text after it is an error and never "a build with no release to compare", because `billet\n  go go1.26.0` once read as a development build and the downgrade judgement was skipped over it (2026-09-10).

**The guard record carries a `note`.** `--note` on `prepare --validate` and on `hold`, at most 200 bytes of printable text (`checkNote`), written at acquisition and never rewritten by a validation, a re-binding, a settlement or a takeover; every reader (`checkGuardRecord`, `guard_fallback.py`'s `OPTIONAL_MEMBERS`, `guard-answer.yml`) admits its absence and refuses a non-string. ADR-006's "the guard beside its ancestors" records why it is `note` and not `reason`, and why `--old-driver-stopped` stays an assertion rather than balena's one-shot force.
