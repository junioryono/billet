# The host role

Part of the `billet-infra-terraform-ansible` skill. Each rule is stated in full, with the measurement or incident behind it.

**The host role owns the fail-closed ledger mount and the upgrade transaction.** With `billet_ledger_volume_id` set to the module's output, the role resolves the volume by its NVMe identity (never a filesystem UUID, which a snapshot clone duplicates), formats only when blank, mounts through a systemd unit with no `nofail`, adds `Requires=` and `RequiresMountsFor=` to `billet-server.service`, proves the state directory is served by the expected volume and refuses to shadow a root-disk ledger. Without that, a failed mount lets the controller start on the root disk with a new ledger, identity and CA. `billet_server_prepare_only: true` is the stopping point for moving a control plane: it mounts and proves the volume and leaves both services installed, disabled and refused by their own units, so nothing can mint an identity until the old host's state is copied in; both are held because whichever role starts first mints the identity. Do not `systemctl mask` here, because the role renders the unit at the path masking would write. The upgrade transaction is in `billet-releases-and-upgrades`.

**The role gives way to a rollout, and renders the timers that carry one out.** `billet_automatic_updates` (default true) installs `billet-upgrade.{service,timer}` on a host whose server should run and `billet-images.{service,timer}` on a firecracker host, and enables the timers (installed disabled when false); `tests/unit-parity.yml` compares their directives with `deploy/`. `prepare-exclusion.yml` first clears per-inclusion settlement and answerer facts with a connection-free `set_fact`, retaining a held run's holder, public id and learned claim/recovery facts so reinclusion validates the same guard. The preparation (the role's second task, and every fleet play's first pre-task) refuses a converge when `/var/lib/billet/upgrades/active` is a symlink, which is `billet host-upgrade`'s claim, naming `--status` and `--resume`, classifies a regular file as the pre-R role's claim by its own stat, and otherwise is a THIN CALLER of `billet converge-guard prepare --holder H --json`, TWICE, through one parser (`guard-answer.yml`, every member judged by name, the token kept in a fact no report prints): the first call `--validate` (acquire or validate, before anything is decided or staged), then the staging under the guard, then the second call with the intent (`--candidate <staged> [--allow-downgrade]`, `--no-change`, or `--recovery` when the first answer carried a pointer), then `settle --token` exactly when this inclusion acquired; a refusal between the acquisition and the settlement runs the block's rescue, `release --holder H --cleanup --token T`, which the command refuses for a settled guard, a wrong token or a pointer. Which executable answers: the managed binary; when it predates the guard or is absent and a guard exists, the executable the record names after the `guard_fallback` module proved it (the five members plus `id`, `token` and `preparing`, typed when present); on a bootstrap or a pre-R host with a candidate, the STAGED candidate after the staging (`--expect-bootstrap` on a bootstrap). Every call is bounded by platform (`timeout -k 10` on Linux, the task's `async` bound with no escalation on a Mac) and the calls that carry the token run under `no_log`; a later inclusion sends `--expect-id`; a second answer must carry the pointer the intent was declared from (`--recovery` exactly when the first answer carried one) and, with it, the target the first answer named, and the interruption facts are the first answer's alone, or a second answer that cleared the pointer would send the ordinary path over the recovery directory's material before the pointer refused its publication; check mode runs `prepare --dry-run` before any shape dispatch and then the read-only staging. `stage-candidate.yml` judges nothing about the guard: the channel resolves into the private `billet_resolved_version` (the public pin is never assigned by a task), the recovery directory is allocated by `allocate-recovery.yml`, one `mkdir -m 0700` without `-p` inside a five-attempt loop that draws its suffix from `billet_recovery_dir_suffix_command` under `sh -c` on every attempt (`file: state=directory` accepts an existing directory, so two stagers would share one journal), and the candidate is copied into it; the floor, the downgrade and the intent against the record are the command's, under the lock. The role's transaction still refuses a PostgreSQL host, and a PostgreSQL controller whose binary predates the guard is refused rather than run under the legacy protocol; billet's timer upgrades that host. The claim under the guard is the pointer `active/recovery` (`ln -sT`, outside the transaction block, proved by `readlink`), every `slurp` of `active`'s content is gated by a legacy shape, recovery requires an owned claim by shape before it stops anything, and the finalizer removes the pointer alone and sets the shape back to `guard`. `converge-guard-check.sh` is the gate: THE REAL BINARY AT ITS REAL PATHS inside a per-case mount namespace (`sudo -n unshare -m --propagation private`, a tmpfs backing overlays over `/usr/bin` and `/var/lib`; `plant_root` makes `/var/lib/billet` inside the `/var/lib` overlay, whose host lower and tmpfs upper without xino give a directory and its files different devices, the layout in which `local prepare` once refused its own lock (#123), so every planted case runs the mount rule there; cases that start without the parent keep it absent; nothing outside the namespace sees these changes), the managed binary and every staged candidate RECORDING WRAPPERS around backing binaries built once with `-tags billetgatecrash` and release version stamps (a wrapper logs its argv and invocation number, fires the case's hook `BILLET_GATE_HOOK=<cmd>:<n>:<script>` before its own invocation, runs its backing binary exactly once, captures the real answer privately and prints it, a substitute `BILLET_GATE_ANSWER=<cmd>:<n>:<file>`, or nothing `BILLET_GATE_DROP_ANSWER`; `BILLET_GATE_FAIL=<cmd>:refuse|hang|crash:<seam spec>`), the pre-R and hang fakes as scripts, the unescalated cases run inside their namespace as the gate's invoker through `setpriv`, the simulated-darwin cases on the fake with the role's path overrides and the committed corpus, every assertion from the state the namespace dumps after the play, the captured token grepped for in every case's output; the local container needs `--cap-add SYS_ADMIN --security-opt apparmor=unconfined`; `billet_upgrade_root`, `billet_managed_binary` and `billet_upgrade_root_owner_uid` exist for it. The gate in `host-upgrade-order.yml` counts `billet_enable_server` occurrences in `services.yml`, so a new task gates on `billet_server_should_run`, never on `billet_enable_server`. `tasks/backup.yml` renders and enables `billet-backup.{service,timer}` under that predicate and `billet_backup_timer`, and refuses an effective drop-in on the backup unit the way `account.yml` refuses one on the services (asked of systemd through `DropInPaths` after a reload, status judged), because the ledger fence is rendered into the unit; a consumer that carried the fence as a drop-in removes it before converging with this role.

**The role keeps needrestart away from billet's services.** `tasks/needrestart.yml`, imported in the ordinary block after every refusal and before `account.yml` (whose apt install is itself an upgrade needrestart answers), stats `billet_needrestart_binary` and `billet_needrestart_dir` and, where either exists, creates `conf.d` and renders `needrestart-billet.conf.j2` to `90-billet.conf`, root:root 0644; the template is byte-for-byte `deploy/needrestart.conf` (`deploy/needrestart_test.go` compares them and refuses a Jinja delimiter in it), so a host that installed the package and was then converged sees no change. Unlike the package the role owns the file and puts back an edit. It is skipped on the retained retirement route (`when: not billet_retirement_node_ordinary | bool`), whose writes are admitted one destination at a time; adding it there needs the operation document and the drift catalog. Its register and the two defaults are bound in `retained-drift.json`'s `definitions`, which any new register or default anywhere in the role changes. `tests/needrestart-check.yml` (`scripts/gates/scenario.sh needrestart-check`, the `shape` group, escalated) drives the tasks against a temporary tree.

**cephadm.log has one rotation rule, ceph-common's.** The role installs both `ceph-common` (`account.yml`), whose `/etc/logrotate.d/ceph-common` rotates `/var/log/ceph/*.log`, and cephadm, which writes a stanza for `/var/log/ceph/cephadm.log` to `/etc/logrotate.d/cephadm`; logrotate then refuses ceph-common's whole file as a duplicate and `logrotate.service` fails every night (measured on the reference deployment's Linux node, Ceph 20.2.3, 2026-10-13, where it had failed since cephadm's bootstrap). cephadm writes that file only when it is absent (`cephadmlib/logging.py`, `write_cephadm_logrotate_config`), so `tasks/cephadm-logrotate.yml`, imported just before `ceph.yml` so the file is there before cephadm's bootstrap, keeps it holding a comment and no stanza, root:root 0644. It has no register, and it is skipped on the retained retirement route, so the drift catalog is unchanged; `effective-inputs-order.yml` pins its place among the ordinary imports.

**A converge restarts services, so never drive one from a runner billet manages.** The drain destroys the jobs on that host, including the one running the playbook. The role refuses by default; `billet_allow_converge_from_billet_runner` exists for an operator who has established the runner is backed by another host.

**A node is drained for a configuration change only when billet says the node reads it (`billet node config-change`, #183).** A tier added to a catalogue the hosts share through an anchor, even another provider's that this node cannot run, changed every host's billet.yaml, and the converge restarted (drained) a busy Firecracker node behind every running job for about ninety minutes (measured 2026-09-23). No running node reads `tiers:` or `nodes:`: the catalogue reaches a node in each launch command, `nodes:` is the control plane's policy, and the images refresh reads tiers from the file on its own schedule. So `tasks/node-config-verdict.yml`, right after the check-mode preview of the render, writes the candidate to a run-allocated `tempfile` beside the installed file (removed in `always`) and asks the installed answerer, `billet_exclusion_answerer`, for `node config-change --installed --candidate --json`; `billet_node_config_changed` is the preview's change unless billet answered `keep`. `tasks/node-config-answer.yml` is the one parser, and it matches the answer rather than parsing it: keep is exit 0 with exactly the bytes billet writes for keep, because `from_json` on half an answer raises a templating error and fails the converge where the rule is to restart (Codex, 2026-10-21). A release without the verb, no answerer, a failed call, half an answer, an empty one, an answer that is not JSON and any word but keep all restart, as before, and so does a host that runs the control plane too (the verdict is restart there, because a retirement holds that host's own node to running the file it started with). A dry run skips the verdict and reports the change as a node change, and so does a retiring node's retained converge (`billet_retirement_node_ordinary`): that route admits its commands one by one in `tests/retirement-role-observe.py`, the verdict is not among them, and a retirement holds that node to the file it started with, so the restart it always took stands (found by the retirement parity shards, 2026-10-21). The fact gates the drain request before the install, the owed-drain record, `Make a node restart for a changed config a drain`, the config term of the ordinary restart's `when`, and `node-restart.yml`'s drain requirement; the server's restart is unchanged. The role's rendered billet.yaml begins with `initconfig.RoleRenderedMarker`, which `billet join accept` refuses to edit, and which the verdict reads past like any comment. `tests/fixtures/node-config-change` is written by the Go test (`TestNodeConfigChangeFixtures`); `scripts/gates/scenario.sh node-restart-check` drives the parser with both fixtures and every broken answer, `scripts/gates/scenario.sh macos-node-check` drives the Mac's twin of it the same way, and `tests/retained-drift.json` reviews the new tasks.

**The ordinary node restart is requested, never awaited.** `tasks/node-restart.yml` (imported by services.yml under the name "Restart the billet node after non-binary inputs change", whose `when` the gates pin as text) is a stop and a start, each requested and polled, never one restart: it records `MainPID` and `InvocationID`, runs `systemctl stop --no-block billet-node.service`, polls until no process and no job remain, proves the stop by the stopped invocation's own ending (`tasks/node-ending.yml`, `billet node ending check`, #127), admits the start (`billet node ending admit-start`), runs `systemctl start --no-block billet-node.service`, and polls for the new process. A restart job would start the next process before either question could be asked. Each poll is a short `until` step every `billet_node_restart_poll_interval` seconds until a deadline `billet_node_restart_wait` (3600) after its request on the controller's clock (`'%s' | strftime | int`, epoch seconds: `now(utc=true).timestamp()` reads a naive UTC datetime as local time and moves an hour across a daylight-saving change, measured 2026-09-30 on a Pacific-time Mac as hours off `date +%s`; the retries only cap the loop). The start is proved by active, running, an empty `Job=` and a MainPID other than the recorded one (a node that was not running recorded 0, and has no stop to prove). A `systemd_service: state=restarted` held one SSH session for the whole drain, which has no bound: on the reference deployment on 2026-09-30 the session closed 34 minutes in, the converge failed as unreachable and released its guard, and systemd finished the restart afterwards. Past a deadline the last read decides the words, naming `systemctl show billet-node -p ActiveState,SubState,MainPID` in each: a read without exactly the four properties is could-not-tell, a stop whose process or job remains is "still draining", an active node with a start job queued is "still queued", anything else (failed, a new process never ready) "did not come back"; a poll that lost the connection (`ignore_unreachable`) fails as could-not-tell. On ansible-core 2.21 `retries: N` runs N+1 attempts, sleeping between them without subtracting run time, and an exhausted `until` under `failed_when: false` is not failed (measured 2026-09-30), so the loop ends on the deadline rather than a count and the judgement is the tasks after the poll. `tests/node-restart-check.yml` (`scripts/gates/scenario.sh node-restart-check`, the `shape` group) drives it against a scripted systemctl that refuses a restart and a waiting stop or start, and a scripted `billet` answering from the Go-written fixtures (`tests/fixtures/node-ending`); the whole-role fake rotates a restarted unit between two live pids (`BILLET_GATE_SERVICE_PID_NEXT`), because the proof needs a new one.

**Every node stop the role performs is proved by the node's ending, and every node start is admitted.** `tasks/node-ending.yml` asks `billet node ending check|admit-start` through each executable and configuration a caller names, in order (`billet_ending_with`, a list of `exe` and `config`: the stopped node's own release first), by default this converge's answerer (`billet_exclusion_answerer`) with the installed configuration, and parses the first answer with the strict `tasks/ending-answer.yml`, which holds the answer to the question asked: the invocation named (none for a standing check) and the drain required. A release that refuses the word (`unexpected argument "ending"`, which an older `billet node` answers rather than running a node) defers to the next; only when every one does, or none is named, is the node one from before the record, judged by systemd's clean triple with no process and no job, the stated floor, whose refusal names `systemctl reset-failed billet-node`. Any other failure to answer refuses. The network drain's stop, the transaction's stop, recovery's stop and the stop of a node that may not run here require a drain (`--drain`), and each judges a node that had no process to stop without `--invocation`, by what its last process left (the node that leaves reads its record through `billet_ending_state_dir`: the installed configuration's state directory, or the owed drain's once the configuration names no node); every caller refuses, at its read, a running process systemd names no invocation for. A drain the role needs is owed durably: `account.yml` reads `billet_node_drain_owed_path` (`/etc/billet/node-drain-owed`, through `tasks/node-drain-owed.yml`) before the configuration changes, refuses a node configured under any other state directory while one is owed, and records the installed node state directory there before it renders a changed configuration (judged by a check-mode preview of the rendering, which also writes the /run drain request first) or renders the node away; the transaction records it before its stop. While a drain is owed or requested, the ordinary restart runs and requires its stop to have drained, the ordinary start and finalize prove a node with no process to stop drained before starting it, and the transaction judges the owed record (`--state-dir`). Every write of the owed drain is flushed (`sync -f`) before the step it guards, since a rename is atomic but not durable; the restart writes the /run request itself whenever it requires a drain, so an owed drain or a reboot never asks a drain of a stop that was not told to drain; recovery (`upgrade-recover.yml`) reads the owed drain before it rolls back or finalizes, and judges its record. Each check that proves it sets `billet_node_drain_proved`, which every read of the owed drain and every new obligation resets to false, and `services.yml` removes the owed drain only then; the ordinary start of a node not already up, and finalize's start after it stops a readiness probe, pass `admit-start` first.

**The role's variables that must be supplied, never guessed.** `billet_binary_src` or `billet_version` (with `billet_release_url_base` and the channel); `billet_config` (what `billet init --emit ansible` prints); `billet_github_private_key_src` (never replaced implicitly; the role refuses a converge when the managed key path holds nothing) and `billet_github_target_key_srcs` (target name to a local key file for every further `billet_config.targets` entry, installed at `/etc/billet/app-private-key-<name>.pem` under the same owned-versus-foreign rule, computed as one `billet_github_target_keys` list in `tasks/main.yml` and looped in `tasks/account.yml`; `tests/key-policy-check.sh` runs the target cases too); capacity, network ranges, `billet_networks` and guest DNS; `billet_ceph_*` (bootstrap is destructive and never inferred; every disk it may consume is named); `billet_firecracker_version` and checksums. The role installs nothing it cannot verify and refuses to start either service before `billet check` passes.

**The host role migrates a node's endpoint through billet's own commands, and infers nothing from the configurations.** `endpoint-decision.yml` (imported by `account.yml` before the render), `endpoint-migration.yml` (by `services.yml` in place of the ordinary node restart) and `endpoint-receipt.yml` (after the node's start) call `billet node migrate-endpoint --dry-run`, `migrate-endpoint`, `rollout registration` (delegated to `billet_migration_controller`) and `node receipt`; the answers go through `endpoint-answer.yml`, `registration-answer.yml` and `receipt-answer.yml`, one strict parser each, in the shape `guard-answer.yml` set. A first start, a removed node, an absent configuration and a leftover node beside a server-only installation are the command's to report. The executable is `billet_exclusion_answerer`, which `prepare-exclusion.yml` sets in every mode (empty under the legacy shape in a real converge, a pre-R managed binary with no candidate, or no billet; check-mode preparation may publish a non-empty legacy answerer), and without one the role compares endpoints through `junioryono.billet.endpoint_change` (`tests/endpoint_change_check.py`) and refuses a change. The order test pins the decision before the staging and the render, the migration before the restart, the receipt after the start, and the restart's gate on `billet_endpoint_migrated`. A COMMA INSIDE A FLOW-SEQUENCE `argv` IS AN ITEM SEPARATOR: `argv: [systemctl, show, billet-node.service, --property=LoadState,ActiveState,MainPID]` handed systemd `--property=LoadState` and two unit names, which answered `LoadState=not-found` three times and refused every fresh-machine dry run (measured 2026-09-11 on the fleet's guest); quote any argument that carries a comma, and assert the exact argv in the gate (`converge-guard-check.sh`'s E7 does). ANSIBLE RESOLVES `delegate_to` BEFORE IT EVALUATES `when` (measured 2026-09-11: "Empty hostname produced from delegate_to" on a task the block's `when` skipped), so a delegation must template to a host even when the task is skipped; `endpoint-migration.yml` defaults it to `inventory_hostname` and asserts the controller before it. AN ASSERT'S `fail_msg` IS RENDERED BEFORE THE ASSERTION DECIDES, so a member the failing branch has and the passing branch lacks needs a default in the message (the guard's `next`, the migration's `state`). THE HOLDER GRAMMAR HAS ONE PYTHON TWIN, `plugins/module_utils/holder.py`, exposed to templates as `junioryono.billet.valid_holder` (`plugins/filter/holder.py`, a re-export) and imported by `guard_fallback.py` for a record's `taken_over_from`, held to `tests/fixtures/holder-vectors.json`, which `checkHolder`'s Go test writes (`holder_check.py` imports both by the collection's dotted name and requires the filter to expose the shared function); a regular expression counted characters where the command counts bytes and admitted U+FFFD and the C1 controls (round 3 of commit 4c), and the fallback reader's own `startswith("C")` refused a format character such as U+200B that Go's `IsControl` admits, so a chain Go wrote blocked the fallback (commit 5b's first review). THE ROLE'S OUTER BOUNDS around the endpoint commands are sums over the phases and observations the commands may make, from role vars in `vars/main.yml` that `TestTheRoleMirrorsTheEndpointCommandsPhaseBudgets` holds to the Go constants. The decision's dry run lives in `endpoint-dry-run.yml`, so the decision and the runtime-unit repair ask it the same way: a `unit-cannot-hold` answer includes `node-repair.yml`, which refuses inside a retained retirement and under a policy that will not start the node (in check mode too, before it), stops a dry run at the repair's own `--dry-run`, runs `billet node repair-unit --run billet_exclusion_holder` under a bound summed from the migration's phase variables, judges it through the strict `repair-answer.yml`, and asks the decision again from scratch (converge-guard-check E12).

**The scenario tests are scripts/gates/scenario.sh NAME, each one converging the role in a specific state.** `host-upgrade-order` (drain, migration, image, restart and rollback ordering), `unit-parity` (packaged units and the role's templates agree), `ledger-mount-render`, `postgres-profile`, `alert-lifecycle`, `development-check-mode`, `emitted-block-check` (a block `billet init --emit ansible` generated converges), `example-check`, `key-policy-check`, `converge-guard-check` (the guard, and the exclusion's whole preparation against fakes, escalated), `ssh-access-check` (the ssh_access refusals and install against a fake sshd), `cloudflared-connector-check` and `warp-connector-check` (the connector roles against fakes), `fleet-playbook-check` (the shipped playbook's shape and guard), `release-fetch-check`, `firecracker-example-check`, `fetch-retry-check` (every `get_url`/`uri` in both roles carries `timeout`, `register`, `until`, `retries` and `delay`, driven by the `billet_fetch_*` and `billet_development_fetch_*` defaults; one stalled connection once failed a converge behind an approval gate), `hybrid-emission-check` (the inventory and playbook `billet init hybrid` writes pass Ansible's syntax check and `ansible-inventory --list` proves the tier catalogue crossed the anchor onto both hosts), `host-fresh-check` (the documented first step against a fresh machine), `needrestart-check` (the needrestart exclusion, absent, installed, idempotent and restored). CI's `host-lifecycle` job runs them all; `host-upgrade-live.sh` is the destructive one. CI builds billet once per run for every group (the `gate-binaries` job, uploaded as a one-run artifact): `tests/gate-binaries.sh` is the one definition of the guard gate's four stamped `billetgatecrash` binaries, which `converge-guard-check.sh` builds itself on a laptop or, given `BILLET_GATE_PREBUILT`, verifies (the version each reports and the tag, never the VCS stamp, which does not follow a git worktree) and copies; the ordinary CLI reaches the other gates through `BILLET_EMITTED_BLOCK_BINARY` and `BILLET_HYBRID_CHECK_BINARY`. `provenance-check` (#91) converges the role's own `binary.yml`, `record-provenance.yml` and `backfill-provenance.yml` against the real billet and a real manifest written by `scripts/mkreleasemanifest`, served from a local origin, inside a private mount namespace whose `/usr/bin`, `/var/lib` and `/etc` are tmpfs-backed overlays; it needs passwordless sudo, and CI runs it in `host-lifecycle (policy)`.

**The collection's `galaxy.yml` carries the release version.** A collection installed from a Git tag reports the version in that file, so the cut-release workflow rewrites it.

**Every JSON a role consumes has fixtures written by the Go test that produces it.** `tests/fixtures/<command>/*.json` for `guard-status`, `guard-prepare`, `node-migrate-endpoint`, `node-repair-unit`, `rollout-registration`, `node-receipt`, `rollout-status` and `release-inspect` are the commands' own answers over planted shapes (cmd/billet's `TestThe*FixturesAreTheCommandsOwn`), with paths, identifiers, digests and times spelled as the packaged host's; `fixtureSetIs` refuses a committed file no producer writes and a producer no file matches. A gate's fake answers from those files and never from a shape written by hand; a role-side parser is written against the complete object of every fixture, nullability per branch included (a migration's `unchanged` has a null `endpoint` only under `record: none` or `unread`, a `reported` a null `to` only beside `node_removed: true`).

**The role puts jobs' IO ahead of Ceph's scrubbing and recovery (#394).** `ceph.yml` sets `osd_mclock_profile` to `billet_ceph_osd_mclock_profile` (default `high_client_ops`; Ceph's own default is `balanced`), because the cluster exists to serve guests' disks, and on 2026-10-05 a deep scrub ran on OSD drives the trash purge already held near saturation while every job waited. It is a cluster setting for every OSD, applied on every converge, so an operator who wants another profile sets the variable rather than the cluster, and "" leaves a shared cluster's own policy alone; `validate-interpreted-inputs.yml` refuses any other value before the converge changes anything, because a refusal at the Ceph task could come after guest networking had already drained the node.

**The cache pool's copies are their own setting (#394).** `billet_ceph_cache_pool_size` and `billet_ceph_cache_pool_min_size` default to `billet_ceph_pool_size` and its min_size, and a single-host cluster may keep one copy of the cache pool, which holds every job's root disk and the build caches, all of which a job can do without: on the reference deployment's two consumer NVMe drives, two copies of every job write held each drive near 92% busy with 17 jobs running (2026-10-06), and one copy halves it. A size of 1 is said twice, as Ceph requires (`mon_allow_pool_size_one`, then `--yes-i-really-mean-it` on its own task so the ordinary replication task never carries it), and `POOL_NO_REDUNDANCY` is never muted, because Ceph can only mute it for the whole cluster, which would hide a pool left at one copy by mistake: the role unmutes it on every converge, and `ceph-health.sh` drops it only while every pool it names is one this inventory chose to keep in one copy, then treats a run left with nothing to report like HEALTH_OK, or its state would never clear and a returning problem would never be mailed again. `validate-interpreted-inputs.yml` refuses a copy count outside 1 to 10 (Ceph's own bound), or a min_size above its size, before anything changes.

**Uplink shaping adjusts itself and is on by default (2026-10-08).** The role runs `billet uplink shape` as `billet-uplink.service` (imported after `services.yml` so a restart runs the binary this converge installed, and never on a retiring node's retained converge, which is why the retained-drift catalog has no rows for it); `billet_uplink_shaping: false`, validated as a boolean, stops and removes it. The shaper is `internal/uplink`: CAKE on the default route's interface and on an IFB for ingress, each direction starting at 25 Mbit/s, or at what the run before a restart learned (`/var/lib/billet-uplink/learned`, the unit's `StateDirectory`, so a reboot keeps it too, resumed as it was however long the shaper was down; 2026-10-16, #493, and 2026-10-17), and cut only when the reflectors' round trip or the default gateway's own rises, or the line loses the reflectors' probes, while that direction is busy (the gateway's unanswered probes are never loss: the reference node's went unanswered 2% of the time with the line quiet), so a host on a line it does not fill is never slowed. `ExecStopPost=billet uplink clear` removes the qdiscs whatever way the shaper ended. The shaper is a `billet` process, and the binary transaction and its rollback each wait for every `billet` process to end before hiding the executable, so both stop `billet-uplink.service` first, while its cleanup can still run the binary; `upgrade-finalize.yml` starts it again where it is enabled, and a start that fails there is reported by a `debug` and fails nothing (2026-10-16: the shaper is not what was upgraded, and failing the finalize held a committed transaction open over it), and `uplink.yml` starts it afterwards. `billet host-upgrade`, the Go transaction, did not touch the shaper until 2026-10-16: a Linux node moved by a rollout kept its shaper on the deleted old binary until the shaper's own once-a-minute replacement check, which an operator's restart beat; it now restarts a running, enabled shaper last, after a commit and after a rollback (`RestartUplink`). Ownership of what is on the interface comes only from the record under `/run/billet-uplink/`, written after `billet uplink check` passes, and one `flock` there keeps a second shaper or a `clear` from running under a live one. A fixed-rate unit was written first and replaced: it needed the line's speed, which only the operator knows and which changes. Measured on a 500 Mbit/s residential line: the node at 378 down and 180 up gave every device at the site 2.5% loss and latency from 7 to 29 ms; held to 400 each way, no loss in 80 pings from the node or a laptop beside it while the node still pulled 342. Learning from latency alone then failed on the same site (2026-10-14): after a restart onto a new release the shaper ran at 1 Gbit/s and cut nothing for three hours while the node sent up to 888 Mbit/s and a laptop behind the gateway lost its connections, because that gateway drops rather than queues and the round trip rose about 11 ms, under the 15 ms the shaper needs. So `billet_uplink_max_up_mbit` and `billet_uplink_max_down_mbit` (0 by default, rendered as `--max-up`/`--max-down` only when positive) give each direction a ceiling it never rises past; the latency rule still cuts below it. A release without the flags refuses them, so set them only on a host whose billet has them. Loss became a signal of its own on 2026-10-15 (#500): an unanswered probe had been read as a `math.MaxInt64` delay, logged as `delay=2562047h47m16.854775807s`, and loss at a minority of reflectors moved nothing. Now probes lost in 2 of 6 rounds from most of the reflectors cut a busy direction to 0.75 of what it moves, a round with no measurement neither cuts nor raises, and on a 500/500 line a 400 ceiling still swung a Wi-Fi laptop's ping to 25-32 ms, and 150 held about 9.5 ms. Since 2026-10-17 nothing needs the ceiling: each direction keeps where the line was last found full (what it moved at its last cut, persisted beside the rate) and climbs back to nine tenths of it at 4% a step, then 2% at most every ten seconds (the least rise the stepper applies, so the rate what flows is judged against is CAKE's), and near the line or before one is found only while it moves nine tenths of its rate, so a dropping gateway is overrun for the seconds its loss takes to show, now and then, rather than within seconds of every cut. A cut takes what a direction moved as its line only when it moved at least half its rate and a twentieth of the other direction's throughput, since both directions are cut on one verdict and an upload's acknowledgements (2 to 3% of the download) are not its line; half its rate alone failed on the second download, the first cut having left the upload at a rate its acknowledgements fill. A resumed rate starts no higher than nine tenths of the line, and a record without the `format` field counts as nothing learned, since the shaper that wrote it recorded its starting ceiling before measuring anything and decayed toward it. Unless the reflectors confirm a queue by themselves (their delay past Bloat in Detect of the window's rounds, this one included), a loss, a gateway stall or an unconfirmed rise is blamed on a direction only while it moves a tenth of its rate, or of the line it found when lower (`lossShare`): on the first day uncapped two of five cuts took a direction carrying 18 or 19 Mbit/s, about 3% of its line, to about 14 for a loss it had not been shown to cause (#592); a quarter, the first choice, left bursts and LAN-raised rates uncut at four times what moves rather than ten. Every five minutes the run logs `the line since the last report` (rates, lines found, peak and mean throughput, delayed, lossy, stalled and blind rounds, cuts, delay percentiles; `internal/uplink/report.go`), because a run that logged only its cuts said nothing on a day it took none (2026-10-18: eleven hours uncapped on the reference node with no cut and no way to tell from the journal why). The role's two variables stay, 0 by default, for a site that wants a host held lower; a converge that removes them restarts the shaper, which resumes what it learned.
